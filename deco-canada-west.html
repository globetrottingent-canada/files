Décor & Party Guide

BC, Alberta & The Prairies

Pacific salmon and Okanagan wine country, a Stampede white hat and chuckwagon spirit, and a prairie church-hall table of perogies and pysanky.

01

The Visual Spirit

Western Canada spans three genuinely distinct registers, each rooted in its own landscape and immigrant story. British Columbia is Pacific Rim sophistication — cedar-planked salmon, Okanagan wine country, and Vancouver's world-class Cantonese and Japanese-influenced food scene, all wrapped in coastal mountain scenery. Alberta is cowboy country, expressed with total sincerity — cattle ranches, the white Stampede hat, and a rodeo culture over a century old. The Prairies (Saskatchewan and Manitoba) carry a deep Ukrainian, German, and Scandinavian settler heritage — perogies, cabbage rolls, and painted pysanky eggs at church-hall suppers.

Across all three, the underlying spirit is unpretentious pride in exceptional local ingredients and hard-won heritage — the palette runs from Pacific blue-green through foothills gold and Stampede red-and-white to prairie wheat and Ukrainian egg-pattern colour.

02

Setting the Scene

For BC coastal style, set up a cedar-plank salmon station, bring in an Okanagan wine bar (Pinot Noir or Riesling), and lean into natural materials — cedar, driftwood, and mountain-and-ocean imagery. Vancouver's Pacific Rim food scene (dim sum, sushi) makes an easy, genuine addition.

For an Alberta Stampede theme, the white cowboy hat is the icon — hand them out if you can. Add a chuckwagon or campfire feel, a proper Alberta beef station (a well-aged steak, simply done), and a pancake-breakfast spread — a genuine Stampede tradition. Red-and-white, denim, and fringe complete the western look.

For prairie Ukrainian heritage, set the table church-hall style with perogies, cabbage rolls, and borscht, and bring in painted pysanky eggs as décor — a nod to Vegreville's giant pysanka landmark. Keep the mood warm and communal, like a family or community gathering.

03

Dressing the Room

  • BC: set up a cedar-plank salmon station — the First Nations technique that defines Pacific Coast cooking.
  • BC: add an Okanagan wine bar — Pinot Noir or Riesling, genuinely world-class Canadian wine.
  • Alberta: hand out white cowboy hats — the iconic Stampede symbol, given even to visiting dignitaries.
  • Alberta: set up a proper Alberta beef station — a well-aged ribeye, simply grilled, served with pride.
  • Alberta: add a pancake breakfast spread — a genuine, beloved Stampede tradition.
  • Prairies: paint or display pysanky eggs — intricately patterned Ukrainian Easter eggs, a nod to Vegreville's giant landmark.
  • Prairies: lay out a church-hall style spread — perogies, cabbage rolls, and borscht with fresh dill.
  • Any register: keep it unpretentious and proud — Western Canadian hospitality is generous rather than fussy.

Did You Know?

  • The Calgary Stampede's white hat tradition began in 1946, developed with Smithbilt (a Calgary hat maker founded by Russian immigrants in 1919) — it's since been presented to countless visiting dignitaries as a symbol of the city's western hospitality.
  • Alberta is home to one of the world's largest Ukrainian diaspora communities — over 1.2 million Canadians claim Ukrainian heritage, and Vegreville, Alberta hosts the "World's Largest Pysanka," a giant monument to that community's contribution.
  • The California roll — inside-out sushi with avocado, cucumber, and crab — was almost certainly invented in Vancouver in the late 1960s or early '70s by chef Hidekazu Tojo, to ease Canadian diners into raw fish.
  • Cedar-planked salmon is a First Nations technique — slow-cooking salmon on cedar wood over an open fire — representing one of the world's oldest continuous relationships between a culture and a single food species.

06

Room & Table Décor

BC & Alberta

  1. Salmon slow-cooked on cedar wood over an open fire — a genuine, ancient Pacific Coast First Nations preparation.
  2. World-class BC wine, now competing with California and Europe — a genuine point of provincial pride.
  3. The classic white cowboy hat — Calgary's warm-hospitality symbol since 1946, and instantly recognisable.
  4. A well-aged Alberta ribeye, served simply — the pinnacle of the provincial table, eaten with total conviction.

The Prairies

  1. Intricately patterned, hand-decorated Ukrainian Easter eggs — a beautiful nod to the region's deep Ukrainian heritage.
  2. Boiled dumplings with sour cream, and rice-and-meat cabbage rolls baked in tomato sauce — the prairie's edible weekly heritage.
  3. Vibrant magenta beet soup, finished with fresh dill and sour cream — a warming, communal prairie classic.
  4. A simple wheat sheaf as a centrepiece — a nod to the enormous grain-growing prairie landscape.
07

What to Wear

BC & Alberta

  1. Relaxed, outdoorsy layers — the easygoing, nature-focused style of the BC coast.
  2. The classic white hat with denim and boots — genuine Calgary Stampede attire, worn with real pride.
  3. Fringe jackets and western-cut shirts — still fashionable and fun at Stampede time.

The Prairies

  1. A traditional embroidered Ukrainian shirt — a genuine, proud heritage garment still worn at prairie community events.
  2. Comfortable, practical clothing — the honest, no-fuss style of the prairie community table.
08

Music & Sound

BC & Alberta

  1. Relaxed, easygoing indie and acoustic music — a natural fit for BC's laid-back Pacific coast mood.
  2. Classic country music — the near-universal soundtrack of a Calgary Stampede celebration.
  3. A lively, hollered "Yahoo!" energy — evoking the chuckwagon races and rodeo announcing that defines the Stampede's spirit.

The Prairies

  1. Accordion-driven Ukrainian folk music — a genuine, deeply rooted part of prairie community life.
  2. Cheerful polka music, a legacy of the region's German, Ukrainian, and Scandinavian settlers, still played at community dances.
