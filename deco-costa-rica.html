CENTRAL AMERICA / TICO

Décor & Party Guide

Costa Rica

Pura vida — hand-painted oxcarts, marimba and folk skirts twirling, fresh-brewed coffee, and rainforest green at every turn.

01

The Visual Spirit

Pura vida — "pure life" — is more than a saying in Costa Rica; it's a whole outlook of gratitude, ease, and appreciation for the natural world, and it should be the guiding feeling of the party. The country's most beloved craft symbol is the carreta, the hand-painted oxcart of Sarchí, its wheels and body covered in swirling floral mandalas in bright, jewel-like colour — now a UNESCO-recognized art form and a fixture in nearly every Tico household.

Layer that with rich Central Valley coffee culture, the rolling marimba music and swirling skirts of Guanacaste's cowboy (sabanero) country, and the sheer biodiversity of the rainforest — toucans, sloths, and impossible greens. The palette runs to carreta brights (red, blue, yellow, green) set against natural wood and tropical foliage, with coffee brown and cloud-forest mist rounding it out.

02

Setting the Scene

Make a carreta-style mandala the visual anchor — a real miniature painted oxcart if you can find one, or simply the swirling floral pattern reproduced on a tablecloth, banner, or paper decorations. Set up a proper coffee station (Costa Rica is one of the world's great coffee-growing countries), and bring in lush tropical greenery, orchids, and toucan or sloth imagery for the rainforest note. Keep the wood natural and the colour saturated rather than pastel.

For a livelier, folkloric feel, lean into Guanacaste: marimba music, the swirling punto guanacasteco dance, and sabanero cowboy touches — wide-brimmed hats, leather, and horse imagery. A patron-saint-festival (fiesta patronal) atmosphere — string lights, a small carnival-fair feel, and fireworks-adjacent sparkle — rounds out the celebratory side.

03

Dressing the Room

  • Centre the room on a painted oxcart (carreta) motif — swirling floral mandalas in bright jewel colour, Costa Rica's most beloved craft symbol.
  • Set up a proper coffee station — Costa Rican coffee is world-class, and a "cafecito" corner doubles as décor and hospitality.
  • Bring in lush tropical greenery — ferns, orchids, and big leafy plants for the rainforest note.
  • Add toucan, sloth, and hummingbird imagery — Costa Rica's biodiversity is a genuine point of national pride.
  • Use natural wood throughout — furniture, serving boards, and accents — to ground the bright carreta colour.
  • (Guanacaste/folkloric) add sabanero cowboy touches — wide-brimmed hats, leather, and horse imagery — plus swirling folk skirts for the punto guanacasteco.
  • String warm lights and bunting for a fiesta-patronal, small-town-festival feel.
  • Nod to Chorotega pottery or Boruca masks for genuine indigenous-heritage craft touches.

05

Did You Know?

  • The painted oxcart tradition is recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage — carts once used to haul coffee to market are now covered in intricate, brightly coloured mandalas, a craft centred in the town of Sarchí.
  • Each boyero (oxcart driver) traditionally wears a white shirt, wide-brimmed hat, and red bandana — and the oxen themselves are decked in colourful ribbons and bells for parade days.
  • The marimba is inseparable from Guanacaste's identity, providing the rhythm for the punto guanacasteco, the country's signature folk dance of swirling skirts and embroidered shirts.
  • The Danza de los Diablitos in Boruca is a three-day collective ritual performance, and the Tope Nacional horse parade brings richly dressed sabaneros and their mounts through the streets of San José — both genuine, living folk traditions rather than tourist shows.

06

Room & Table Décor

Craft & Colour

  1. A miniature painted oxcart, or the swirling mandala pattern reproduced elsewhere — Costa Rica's most iconic and beloved folk-art symbol.
  2. Hand-shaped pottery with intricate indigenous designs from the Guaitil area — an authentic craft tradition centuries deep.
  3. Costa Rican coffee, brewed properly (a chorreador cloth-filter setup is a lovely touch) — both décor and genuine national pride.
  4. Carved wooden masks and woven textiles from the Boruca people, used in the Danza de los Diablitos — powerful, symbolic indigenous art.

Nature & Setting

  1. Lush ferns, orchids, and big leafy plants — the rainforest abundance that's central to Costa Rican identity.
  2. Toucans, sloths, and hummingbirds as prints or small figures — genuine national pride in the country's astonishing wildlife.
  3. Unfinished or lightly finished wood furniture and serving pieces — a calm base for the bright carreta colour to sit against.
  4. String lights and bunting for a warm, community-fair feel — the atmosphere of a Costa Rican patron-saint festival.
07

What to Wear

For Her

  1. A full, flowing skirt and embroidered blouse in bright colour — the traditional dress for Guanacaste's marimba-driven folk dance.
  2. A bright tropical flower tucked into the hair — an easy, genuine finishing touch.
  3. A simple, colourful cotton dress — comfortable and right at home at any Tico gathering.
  4. Woven straw or textile accessories with a Chorotega-inspired pattern — a small, genuine craft touch.

For Him

  1. A wide-brimmed hat, plain shirt, and leather boots — the working sabanero cowboy look of Guanacaste ranching country.
  2. A white shirt, wide-brimmed hat, and red bandana — the traditional dress of the oxcart driver, tying directly into the carreta theme.
  3. A light, breathable shirt in a bright or embroidered style — comfortable and appropriate for the climate and mood.
  4. A simple woven straw hat — easy, comfortable, and right for the pura vida mood.
08

Music & Sound

Folk & Traditional

  1. The wooden xylophone-like marimba is inseparable from Guanacaste's identity — bright, rolling, and instantly festive.
  2. Costa Rica's signature folk dance — lively, marimba-driven, and performed in colourful traditional dress at festivals nationwide.
  3. A distinct Afro-Caribbean calypso style from Limón, on the Caribbean coast — a genuinely different, equally Costa Rican regional sound.
  4. Drums and conch-shell horns accompanying the Boruca people's three-day theatrical ritual — profound, living indigenous tradition.

Modern & Ambient

  1. Contemporary Costa Rican pop and Latin hits for an easy, upbeat modern playlist.
  2. Birdsong, rain, and jungle ambience as a soft background layer — leaning into the pura vida connection with nature.
  3. Widely loved across Central America — an easy way to keep a mixed crowd dancing.
  4. Simple, warm acoustic guitar for a relaxed, conversational pura vida evening.
