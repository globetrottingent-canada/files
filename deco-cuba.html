CARIBBEAN / CUBAN

Décor & Party Guide

Cuba

Old Havana at golden hour — faded pastel walls, candy-coloured classic cars, string lights and a mojito, and son and salsa spilling into the street.

01

The Visual Spirit

A Cuban party is warm, worn, and soaked in music — the golden glow of old Havana. Picture faded pastel colonial walls with a peeling patina, candy-coloured 1950s American cars at the kerb, string lights overhead, a mojito sweating in your hand, and live music never far away. The mood is sultry, unhurried, and romantic, and music and dance sit at the very centre of everything.

It's a blend of roots — Spanish melody and African rhythm in the music (son, rumba), Art Deco and Spanish-colonial in the architecture, and lush Caribbean tropics all around. The palette runs to Havana pastels (turquoise, coral, mint, buttercup), the flag's blue-white-red, rum amber and tobacco brown, and plenty of crisp white — a classic Cuban party colour. Scatter in vintage records, cigar boxes, and old travel posters.

The look, and the living island

Golden-age Havana — the "Havana Nights" image most people reach for: vintage cars, the Buena Vista Social Club, mojitos, cigars, faded pastel glamour. Beloved, romantic, and largely authentic.

Living Cuba — the island is also modern and moving, with timba, reggaeton, and a contemporary scene well beyond the 1950s postcard. Lean here for present-day rather than nostalgic.

One note of respect: Afro-Cuban Santería — the orishas and batá drums — is a living religion, not décor. Enjoy the rumba and folklore, but don't turn sacred objects into props.

02

Setting the Scene

Build old Havana. Hang warm, dim string lights for that sultry street glow, and lean into faded-colonial pastels — or vintage Cuban travel posters and prints (Wifredo Lam, Amelia Peláez). Bring in tropical palms and bright flowers, make a rum-and-mojito bar the centrepiece, and scatter vintage records and cigar boxes around. Above all, leave a clear floor — dancing is non-negotiable.

Put music at the heart. Set out real percussion — congas, bongós, claves, maracas, and a güiro — for guests to pick up, and keep son and salsa playing (live, if you can manage it). Give one corner to a domino table and another to cigars, and rig a Malecón-seawall or classic-car backdrop for photos. The room should feel like a street party that could break out at any moment.

03

Dressing the Room

  • Hang warm string lights — low and golden — for the sultry glow of a Havana street at night.
  • Go pastel and faded-colonial — turquoise, coral, mint, and buttercup, ideally with a lived-in, peeling-paint patina.
  • Bring in the tropics — palm and banana leaves and bright flowers for a lush backdrop.
  • Add vintage Havana props — old records and a record player, cigar boxes, travel posters, and prints by Cuban artists.
  • Nod to the classic cars — a candy-coloured 1950s car backdrop, model, or poster, the icon of the island.
  • Build a rum & mojito bar — Havana Club, fresh mint and lime — with a cigar corner alongside.
  • Set a percussion table — congas, bongós, claves, maracas, and a güiro for guests to play.
  • Leave a dance floor and a domino table — the two things a Cuban gathering simply can't do without.

Did You Know?

  • Son cubano — born in the eastern highlands in the late 1800s, blending Spanish melody with African rhythm — is the root of salsa. The Buena Vista Social Club brought its old masters back to the world stage in the 1990s.
  • Afro-Cuban rumba is a whole family: the slow yambú, the flirtatious couple-dance guaguancó, and the fast, showy columbia — the body language behind much of Cuban dance.
  • Dominoes is practically a national sport, played loudly on street corners and at nearly every gathering. A domino table is as Cuban as a mojito.
  • Las Parrandas de Remedios, held every December since the 18th century, is one of Cuba's oldest festivals — rival neighbourhoods compete with giant illuminated floats and enormous fireworks.

06

Room & Table Décor

Old Havana

  1. Warm, dim string lights and a faded, lived-in backdrop — the sultry, golden atmosphere of an old Havana street after dark.
  2. Turquoise, coral, mint, and buttercup with a peeling-paint patina — the look of Habana Vieja's colonial facades.
  3. Palm and banana leaves and bright tropical flowers to bring the lush, warm Caribbean into the room.
  4. Vintage vinyl and a record player, old cigar boxes, and Cuban travel posters — the old-timey details that sell the era.

Music & Play

  1. Congas, bongós, claves, maracas, and a güiro set out for guests to pick up — décor and impromptu band in one.
  2. Havana Club rum, fresh mint and lime, and plenty of ice for mojitos, daiquiris, and Cuba Libres — with a cigar corner alongside.
  3. A table set for dominoes — the beloved Cuban game. Set it up and a noisy, competitive tournament tends to start itself.
  4. A candy-coloured 1950s car or a Malecón-seawall backdrop for photos — the single most recognisable image of Cuba.
07

What to Wear

For Her

  1. The ruffled, flowing rumba dress — dramatic layered flounces made for spinning on the dance floor.
  2. Crisp white linen or a guayabera dress — cool, elegant, and a very common (and correct) Cuban party choice.
  3. A big bright flower tucked behind the ear — the effortless finishing touch, with bold colour or gold hoops.
  4. A vivid tropical dress and a hand fan (abanico) — practical in the heat and pure old-Havana glamour.

For Him

  1. The classic lightweight, pleated, open-necked shirt worn untucked — the definitive Cuban men's look, in white or a soft colour.
  2. A woven Panama hat or a jaunty fedora — the easiest way to top off the outfit with instant Havana style.
  3. Light linen trousers and two-tone spectator shoes — dapper, breezy, and ready to dance.
  4. A cigar (lit or not) completes the character — Cuba's most famous export and a natural finishing prop.
08

Music & Sound

Son & Roots

  1. The warm, swinging root of Cuban music (and of salsa) — tres guitar, trumpet, and gentle percussion. The heart of a Havana evening.
  2. The easiest way to set the tone — the beloved recordings that reintroduced Cuba's golden-age son masters to the world.
  3. Slow, romantic guitar ballads for the softer moments — the sound of a Havana courtyard late at night.
  4. The improvised, verse-trading country music of the Cuban peasantry — heard at rural guateques and parrandas.

Salsa & Dance

  1. Salsa — and Cuba's own circular partner style, casino — get everyone moving. A short lesson early on pays off all night.
  2. Pure Afro-Cuban percussion and voice — guaguancó and its cousins, driven by congas and the clave rhythm at the core of it all.
  3. The big-band dance crazes of mid-century Havana — brassy, upbeat, and perfect for a retro theme.
  4. Cuba's contemporary, hard-driving dance sound — a modern option if you want present-day energy over nostalgia.
