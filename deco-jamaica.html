CARIBBEAN / JAMAICAN

Décor & Party Guide

Jamaica

One love, easy rhythm — bandana check and bamboo, the bassline of a reggae one-drop, dominoes on the veranda, and jerk smoke on the breeze.

01

The Visual Spirit

Jamaica's spirit is easy, warm, and unhurried — a veranda, a domino game, a plate of jerk, and reggae's unmistakable off-beat rhythm carrying the whole evening along. It's the birthplace of ska, rocksteady, reggae, dancehall, and the deep folk roots of mento and Kumina drumming, plus the island's biggest cultural export: Bob Marley's "one love" philosophy of peace and unity, still felt everywhere.

Visually, that means natural materials (bamboo, wood, woven straw), the national colours of green, gold, and black, and the red-and-white check of Jamaican bandana plaid (its own distinct pattern, different from the French Caribbean's madras). Bright hibiscus and greenery, a relaxed island palette, and a laid-back "no problem" pace tie it all together.

A note on Rastafari

Rastafari's influence on Jamaica is immense — its ites-green-gold colours (drawn from the Ethiopian flag) and its philosophy are woven through reggae and island life. But Rastafari is a living religion, not a costume aesthetic. Enjoy the music, colours, and message of unity, and steer clear of turning sacred symbols (like dreadlocks or ceremonial elements) into a novelty costume.

02

Setting the Scene

Build a relaxed veranda feel: bamboo furniture or screens, string lights, and a hammock corner if there's room. Dress the table in bandana-plaid (the red-and-white check) runners or cloths, and bring in bright hibiscus and tropical greenery. Set up a jerk station or grill as the centrepiece — the smoky pimento-and-scotch-bonnet aroma is half the atmosphere — alongside a rum bar. Keep a domino table going in one corner; it's a genuine, beloved pastime, not a prop.

Sound matters as much as sight: reggae's rolling one-drop bassline should be present all evening, whether live or on a good speaker. For a festive touch, nod to Jonkonnu — the Christmas masquerade of colourful costumed characters and drumming — with bright, patchwork-inspired costume elements and a bit of playful theatre.

  • Bring in green, gold, and black — the national colours — plus bright hibiscus and tropical greenery.
  • (Festive) nod to Jonkonnu — colourful, patchwork-inspired costume touches and drumming for a Christmas or festival theme.
  • Play reggae throughout — the rolling one-drop rhythm should be the backbone of the whole evening's mood.

Did You Know?

  • Jamaica is the birthplace of ska, rocksteady, reggae, and dancehall — an extraordinary run of musical innovation from one small island, with mento as the folk root that started it all.
  • Jonkonnu is a Christmas masquerade dating back to the 1600s — costumed characters like Pitchy-Patchy and Househead parade door to door between Christmas and New Year, blending African and British influences.
  • Jamaica owes much of its African-heritage preservation to the Maroon communities — descendants of enslaved Africans who won their freedom and autonomy in the mountains, keeping traditions like Kumina drumming alive.
  • The doctor bird (swallow-tail hummingbird) is Jamaica's national bird, found nowhere else in the world, and the lignum vitae — one of the hardest woods on Earth — is the national flower and tree.

06

Room & Table Décor

The Veranda

  1. Bamboo furniture or screens with warm string lights — the relaxed, natural feel of a Jamaican veranda evening.
  2. Jamaica's own red-and-white checked fabric — used for tablecloths, headwraps, and traditional dress, distinct from other islands' madras.
  3. A table set for dominoes — a beloved, noisy, competitive Jamaican tradition that draws a crowd on its own.
  4. Bright hibiscus and lush tropical greenery, loose and generous, for genuine island colour.

Food & Fire

  1. A grill or smoker for jerk chicken or pork — the pimento-wood smoke and scotch bonnet aroma are as much atmosphere as food.
  2. Jamaican rum, ginger beer, and lime for classics like rum punch and a Dark 'n' Stormy — the social hub of the gathering.
  3. Flags, bunting, or simple colour accents in Jamaica's own national colours — a proud, straightforward decorating choice.
  4. Bright, patchwork-patterned costume elements for a Christmas or festival theme — a nod to the centuries-old masquerade tradition.
07

What to Wear

For Her

  1. A dress or wrap in Jamaica's red-and-white bandana check — a genuinely national look, often worn at cultural and folk events.
  2. A folded headwrap in bandana check or bright island print — a simple, elegant traditional accent.
  3. A colourful cotton sundress — breezy, simple, and right for a relaxed veranda gathering.
  4. Wooden bangles and shell jewellery — simple, natural accents that suit the island's laid-back materials palette.

For Him

  1. A light, open linen shirt in a bright colour or bandana print — comfortable and effortlessly Jamaican.
  2. A bandana-check sash or pocket square — a small, genuine nod to national dress without overreach.
  3. Simple green, gold, and black clothing or accessories — straightforward national pride, no costume needed.
  4. A woven straw hat — relaxed, practical, and at home on any Jamaican veranda.
08

Music & Sound

The Reggae Family Tree

  1. The unmistakable off-beat "one-drop" groove, carried worldwide by Bob Marley — the definitive Jamaican sound and message of unity.
  2. The upbeat, horn-driven ska of the early 1960s and the slower, soulful rocksteady that followed — reggae's direct ancestors.
  3. Acoustic folk music blending African and European elements, played on banjo, hand drums, and rhumba box — where it all began.
  4. Reggae's electronic-influenced, high-energy modern evolution — the sound (and dance styles) of a contemporary Jamaican party.

Folk & Drumming

  1. Powerful African-rooted drumming and dance kept alive by Maroon communities — a deep, ancestral folk tradition.
  2. Rastafari sacred drumming — profound and meaningful within its own religious context; appreciate its influence rather than reenacting it as party entertainment.
  3. Fife, drum, and grater rhythms accompanying the Jonkonnu masquerade — festive, satirical, and centuries old.
  4. The easiest, most beloved way to set a Jamaican tone for any gathering — warm, uplifting, and universally known.
