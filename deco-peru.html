SOUTH AMERICA / ANDEAN

Décor & Party Guide

Peru

Inca gold and Andean weaving meet coastal sway — Inti Raymi splendour, pisco poured for a toast, and the handkerchief flourish of the Marinera.

01

The Visual Spirit

Peru holds two rich veins of celebration, both equally authentic. The Andean highlands carry the deep legacy of the Inca empire and living Quechua and Aymara culture — hand-woven textiles in bold geometric pattern, alpaca wool, chullo hats, and the sweeping spectacle of Inti Raymi, the Inca sun festival still re-enacted each June at Sacsayhuamán. The coast carries a different, sultry elegance — the swirling handkerchief courtship of the Marinera dance, pisco (Peru's national spirit), and a Spanish-colonial polish.

Both share a love of colour, craft, and genuine ceremony, so a Peruvian party can lean into either or blend them: gold and sun-motifs with Andean weaving for the highland register, or lace, pisco, and dancer's elegance for the coastal one. Either way, the materials are natural and handmade — alpaca wool, silver filigree, clay, and woven textile — and the mood is proud rather than kitsch.

02

Setting the Scene

For the Andean/Inti Raymi register, dress the table in bold woven textile runners, bring in gold and sun-disc imagery, and set out chullo hats and alpaca throws as texture. Candlelight and warm amber tones evoke the sacred, ceremonial feel of the festival, and llama or alpaca figures make a charming, genuine centrepiece.

For the coastal/Marinera register, aim for something more elegant — white linen, lace, silver accents, and a proper pisco bar (pisco sour, made with egg white, lime, and bitters, is the signature). Leave a clear space for dancing; the Marinera's flirtatious handkerchief choreography is meant to be watched and joined in.

03

  • Add alpaca wool textures — throws, chullo hats, and ponchos for warmth and authentic highland material.
  • Set up a pisco bar — pisco sour (egg white, lime, bitters) is Peru's national cocktail and a natural centrepiece.
  • Dress the table in white linen and lace for the coastal, Marinera-elegant register.
  • Light it with candles and warm amber tones for a ceremonial, sacred-festival feel.
  • Add llama or alpaca figures as a charming, genuine centrepiece touch.

05

Did You Know?

  • Inti Raymi, the Festival of the Sun, is a re-enactment of an ancient Inca ceremony honouring Inti, the sun god — held every June 24th at Sacsayhuamán, with hundreds of performers in feathered headdresses and gold-toned costumes.
  • The pollera, the wide layered skirt worn by Andean women, varies in colour, embroidery, and number of layers by region — a skilled eye can often tell exactly which community a woman is from just by her skirt.
  • The Marinera is a courtship dance performed with a waved handkerchief — flirtatious, elegant, and one of Peru's proudest cultural exports, especially strong on the coast.
  • The Virgen de la Candelaria festival in Puno is UNESCO-recognised and blends Aymara and Quechua dance traditions with Catholic devotion and ancestral Pachamama (Mother Earth) rituals — a striking example of Peru's religious syncretism.
Décor & Ambiance Reference

Two registers, one country — Andean splendour and coastal elegance.

06

Room & Table Décor

Andean & Inti Raymi

  1. Bold, colourful hand-woven textiles with geometric Andean motifs — genuine Quechua and Aymara craft, centuries in the making.
  2. Gold tones and sun-disc imagery, echoing Inti Raymi's splendour and the Inca reverence for the sun.
  3. Knitted chullo hats with earflaps and soft alpaca throws — warm, tactile, and genuinely Andean.
  4. Small carved or woven llama and alpaca figures — a gentle, genuine Andean touch for the table.

Coastal & Marinera

  1. Crisp white linen with lace trim — the elegant, Spanish-colonial-influenced coastal register.
  2. Pisco, lime, egg white, and bitters for a proper pisco sour bar — Peru's most celebrated drink and a natural social hub.
  3. Fine silver filigree serving pieces or accents — a refined material with a long Peruvian craft tradition.
  4. Warm candlelight for either register — ceremonial for Inti Raymi, romantic for a Marinera-style evening.
07

What to Wear

For Her

  1. A wide, pleated, colourfully embroidered skirt worn in layers — traditional Andean women's dress, its patterns often signalling home community.
  2. A decorated formal hat, its shape and trim varying by region — often finished with pom-poms and ribbon.
  3. A woven blanket-shawl with traditional Incan-inspired designs, pinned at the shoulder — practical, beautiful highland dress.
  4. A flowing, elegant dress with a handkerchief in hand — the coastal look for the flirtatious Marinera dance.

For Him

  1. A red-and-black or multicoloured striped poncho, worn over a shirt — classic Andean men's dress, especially in the Cusco region.
  2. The knitted wool hat with earflaps and often a tassel — instantly recognisable and genuinely practical in the high Andes.
  3. A crisp white shirt and dark trousers with a handkerchief — the coastal Marinera dancer's look, elegant and simple.
  4. Feathered headdress and gold-toned tunic in the style of Inti Raymi performers — best reserved for an explicitly festival-themed night, worn with pride rather than as generic costume.
08

Music & Sound

Andean

  1. The reed panpipes at the heart of Andean folk music — haunting, melodic, and instantly recognisable.
  2. A notched-end reed flute with a breathy, evocative tone — a staple of highland folk ensembles.
  3. A lively highland folk genre and dance, performed at festivals to honour the agricultural calendar and mountain spirits.
  4. Drums and wind instruments accompanying the sun-festival re-enactment — grand, ceremonial, and steeped in Inca ritual.

Coastal & Modern

  1. Guitar and cajón-driven music for Peru's elegant, flirtatious handkerchief courtship dance.
  2. A wooden box drum, played while seated — an Afro-Peruvian instrument now central to Peruvian music of all kinds.
  3. The romantic, guitar-driven "música criolla" songbook of the coast — waltzes and ballads full of nostalgia and elegance.
  4. Peru's own psychedelic-tinged cumbia (chicha), blending Andean melody with tropical rhythm — a distinctive, danceable modern option.
