Décor & Party Guide

Québec

A sugar shack in the snow, Bonhomme's red toque and arrow sash, and a caribou passed hand to hand under the winter stars.

01

The Visual Spirit

Québec's identity was forged by four centuries of French colonial life adapted to brutal winters, and its décor spirit runs through two beloved, warmly overlapping registers. The cabane à sucre (sugar shack) is rustic habitant warmth — red-checkered cloths, worn wood, maple everywhere, and a big communal feast at the height of sugaring season. The Carnaval de Québec is the festival version: Bonhomme's cheerful red toque and woven arrow sash, an ice palace, and caribou passed around to fight off the cold — one of the world's largest winter carnivals.

Montréal adds its own thread — smoked meat and wood-fired bagels in the Jewish Ashkenazi deli tradition, urban and unpretentious. Across all three, the palette runs to habitant red, maple amber, snow white, and Carnaval blue, with materials staying rustic and warm: worn wood, wool, and wrought iron.

02

Setting the Scene

For a cabane à sucre feast, lay out red-and-white checkered cloths, rustic wood tables, and a big communal spread — maple-glazed ham, baked beans, pork rinds, and crêpes, finished with tire d'érable (maple taffy poured onto snow or crushed ice). Keep the room warm, wood-panelled, and unfussy — this is a farmhouse feast, not fine dining.

For a Carnaval de Québec theme, bring in Bonhomme's signature red-and-white — a red toque, an arrow-patterned sash (ceinture fléchée), and touches of ice-palace blue-white. Serve caribou (a warming, fortified port-and-spirits blend) as the signature drink, and let string lights and fire-pit warmth stand in for the festival's real ice sculptures and bonfires.

For Montréal urban flavour, set up a smoked meat and bagel spread in the classic deli style — simple, generous, and proudly unpretentious, echoing the city's own Jewish Ashkenazi deli heritage.

03

Dressing the Room

  • Lay down red-and-white checkered cloths and rustic wood — the classic cabane à sucre table.
  • Bring in maple everywhere — syrup bottles, maple-leaf motifs, and amber tones throughout.
  • Dress in Bonhomme's colours — red toque, and an arrow-patterned ceinture fléchée sash as a table runner or wall hanging.
  • Serve caribou — the Carnaval's iconic warming, fortified drink — as the signature toast.
  • Add ice-palace blue-white touches and string lights for a festival-night glow.
  • Set up a smoked meat and bagel spread for Montréal's own deli tradition.
  • Bring in wool and wrought iron — warm, practical winter materials throughout.
  • Finish with tire d'érable (maple taffy) — pour onto crushed ice for an easy indoor version of the classic snow treat.

Did You Know?

  • Québec produces roughly 72% of the world's maple syrup, managed through a strategic reserve so significant that its 2012 theft — the "Great Maple Syrup Heist" of roughly 3,000 tonnes worth $18 million — remains one of history's most unusual commodity crimes.
  • The ceinture fléchée (arrow sash) worn by Bonhomme Carnaval has roots in both French-Canadian and Indigenous finger-weaving traditions — voyageurs and fur traders wore it to cinch clothing against the cold, and it was designated intangible Québec heritage in 2012.
  • Bonhomme Carnaval, the festival's beloved seven-foot snowman mascot, has appeared every year since the Carnaval began in 1955 — his effigies are collectible, and the 2025 model was made from recycled maple tubing.
  • The Montréal bagel is genuinely distinct from New York's — smaller, denser, sweetened with honey rather than malt, and wood-fired — with the Mile End's two dynasties, Fairmount (1919) and St-Viateur (1957), keeping up a friendly, decades-long rivalry.

06

Room & Table Décor

Cabane à Sucre

  1. Red-and-white checkered tablecloths over worn, rustic wood tables — the classic sugar shack look, warm and unfussy.
  2. Bottles of maple syrup and maple-leaf motifs as décor — the ingredient that's become synonymous with Québec itself.
  3. Hot maple syrup poured over crushed ice and rolled onto a stick — the most joyful, participatory Québécois tradition there is.
  4. A big shared spread of maple-glazed ham, baked beans, and crêpes — the generous, family-style centrepiece of the sugar shack meal.

Carnaval & Montréal

  1. The colourful woven arrow sash worn by Bonhomme Carnaval — a genuine piece of French-Canadian and Indigenous craft heritage.
  2. A fortified port-and-spirits blend served warm — the iconic drink of the Québec Winter Carnival, meant to fight off the cold.
  3. Blue-white lighting and string lights, echoing Bonhomme's ice palace and the festival's outdoor magic.
  4. Piled-high smoked meat on rye and wood-fired Montréal bagels — the city's own proud deli institution.
07

What to Wear

For Her

  1. A simple red knit toque — Bonhomme Carnaval's signature colour, easy and instantly recognisable.
  2. A colourful woven arrow sash tied at the waist — genuine Québécois heritage worn with pride at Carnaval.
  3. A cosy wool sweater or shawl — practical, warm, and true to the region's rustic winter life.

For Him

  1. A red toque with a ceinture fléchée sash — the exact look of Bonhomme Carnaval himself.
  2. A rustic plaid or leather jacket, evoking the fur-trading coureurs des bois of Québec's early history.
  3. Warm wool and flannel layers — simple, comfortable, and genuinely suited to Québec's long winters.
08

Music & Sound

Traditional Québécois

  1. Fiddle-driven traditional music, heard at Carnaval and cabane à sucre gatherings alike — lively, folk-rooted, and communal.
  2. Québec's beloved traditional-folk revival band — a genuine, widely-loved entry point into the sound.
  3. The French-language singer-songwriter tradition — warm, poetic, and central to Québécois identity.

Modern & Montréal

  1. Contemporary French-Canadian pop and rock — an easy, upbeat modern option for any gathering.
  2. Montréal's celebrated indie music scene — a modern, artsy counterpoint to the traditional Québécois sound.
  3. Cheerful festival music associated with the Carnaval — upbeat and communal, built for a crowd.
