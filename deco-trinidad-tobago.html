CARIBBEAN / TRINIDADIAN

Décor & Party Guide

Trinidad & Tobago

The greatest show on earth — feathers and steelpan, calypso and soca, mas bands on the road, and the paint-soaked joy of J'ouvert.

01

The Visual Spirit

Trinidad's signature is Carnival — proudly billed as "the greatest show on earth" — and it's total sensory maximalism: towering feathered costumes (mas), the ring and clang of steelpan, and calypso and soca driving every foot in the street. It grew out of resistance and celebration, from 18th-century masquerade balls through the defiant post-emancipation Canboulay processions to today's mas bands and Panorama steelband competition. The energy is euphoric, communal, and completely unfiltered.

Design-wise, that means bold, saturated colour on everything — feathers, sequins, beads — set against the flag's red, black, and white, plus steel-drum silver and calypso-tent gold. Bring in real percussion, real feathers, and leave space to dance; restraint is not really the assignment here.

One island, more than one celebration

Carnival (this page's focus) — mas, steelpan, calypso and soca, J'ouvert. Trinidad and Tobago's most famous export and the default for a Trinidadian party.

Divali — thanks to a large Indo-Trinidadian community, Divali is Trinidad's second-biggest national festival: thousands of diyas, arched bamboo light displays, and chutney music. A beautifully different, quieter register worth knowing about if that's the occasion.

Hosay — an Indo-Caribbean Muslim commemoration with colourful model tomb processions (tadjah), now embraced across Trinidad's communities as part of the national culture.

02

Setting the Scene

Go bright and go big. Drape feather boas, sequinned fabric, and beaded fringe everywhere, hang strings of carnival-colour flags or bunting, and set the palette to red, black, gold, and rainbow brights. Bring in a steelpan (even a small one) as a centrepiece, or at least the image of one, and set up a rum punch and Caribbean rum bar. Leave the middle of the floor clear — Carnival is meant to be danced, not just looked at.

If you're leaning Divali instead, the scene flips: warm and glowing rather than loud. Line paths and staircases with rows of diyas (small clay oil lamps), arch bamboo into shapes strung with lights, add rangoli-style floor patterns, and keep the mood serene, sweet-scented, and vegetarian.

03

Dressing the Room

  • Go maximal with feathers and sequins — boas, plumes, and sparkle everywhere. Carnival costumes (mas) are built to be seen from a distance.
  • Hang carnival-colour bunting and flags — bold, saturated, and plentiful.
  • Bring in a steelpan — even a small or toy one as a centrepiece; it's Trinidad's national instrument and unmistakable at a glance.
  • Set up a rum bar — Trinidadian rum, sorrel, and rum punch, garnished with tropical fruit.
  • Add moko jumbie imagery — the towering stilt-walker character — as art, cut-outs, or a hired performer for real drama.
  • Leave a clear dance floor — Carnival is participatory, not a spectator sport. The room should invite movement.
  • (Divali) line paths with diyas and bend bamboo arches strung with lights; add rangoli patterns and marigolds.
  • (Hosay) nod to the colourful tadjah processional structures if that's specifically the occasion being marked.

05

Did You Know?

  • The steelpan was invented in Trinidad in the 1930s, made from discarded oil drums after colonial authorities banned traditional drumming — it's now the country's national instrument and the star of the Panorama competition.
  • J'ouvert ("opening of the day") kicks off around 4am on Carnival Monday, with revellers covered in paint, mud, and oil dancing through the streets — a joyful, liberating tradition rooted in the post-emancipation Canboulay processions.
  • Classic mas characters include the towering stilt-walking Moko Jumbie, the top-hatted, storytelling Midnight Robber, and the oil-and-chain-wearing Jab Jab — costumes that carry real history of resistance and identity.
  • Divali is Trinidad's second-largest national festival after Carnival — thanks to the country's large Indo-Trinidadian population, it's a public holiday marked by the lighting of thousands of diyas and the Divali Nagar cultural exposition.

06

Room & Table Décor

Carnival Statement Pieces

  1. Big feather plumes, sequinned fabric, and beaded fringe — the maximalist glamour at the heart of Carnival costuming.
  2. A steelpan (or a smaller decorative one) as centrepiece — instantly, unmistakably Trinidadian, and a great conversation piece.
  3. A towering, colourfully-dressed stilt-walking figure — as a cut-out, print, or a hired performer for real showstopping drama.
  4. Bold, saturated flags and bunting strung overhead — simple, cheerful, and instantly festive.

The Bar & Table

  1. Trinidadian rum, rum punch, and sorrel (a spiced hibiscus drink), garnished with tropical fruit — the social centre of the party.
  2. A small steelpan, congas, or a shaker set out for guests to try — a hands-on way to bring the Panorama energy into the room.
  3. Small clay oil lamps lining paths and staircases, and bamboo bent into arches strung with lights — the warm, golden Divali equivalent of the Carnival look.
  4. Bright hibiscus, bird of paradise, and marigold (for a Divali touch) — colourful, fragrant, and works for either celebration.
07

What to Wear

Carnival / Mas

  1. A feathered headdress, sequinned bodysuit, and beaded details — the full "playing mas" look, bright and built to move.
  2. Colourful (washable!) body paint for a J'ouvert-inspired look — playful, messy, and true to the tradition's spirit of freedom.
  3. Body glitter and bead necklaces in Carnival colours — an easy way in without a full costume commitment.
  4. Simple flag-colour outfits for anyone who wants to represent Trinidad and Tobago without a costume.

Divali

  1. Traditional Indian dress worn for Divali in Trinidad — elegant, colourful, and central to the festival's dress code.
  2. A kurta top, sometimes paired with a dhoti — the traditional men's Divali outfit, worn across Trinidad's Indo-Trinidadian community.
  3. A bindi and colourful bangles — small, meaningful finishing touches for the occasion.
  4. Simply wearing fresh, new clothing honours the Divali custom of renewal and a clean start.
08

Music & Sound

Carnival

  1. High-energy, danceable soca is the modern sound of Carnival fetes and the road march — impossible to stand still to.
  2. Full steelband arrangements from the Panorama competition — melodic, driving, and unmistakably Trinidadian.
  3. Calypso's clever, storytelling lyrics over a rolling rhythm — the older, wittier cousin of soca, and a Trinidadian original.
  4. A Trinidadian blend of calypso and spoken word — a homegrown genre with real conscious, rhythmic energy.

Chutney & Divali

  1. A lively fusion of Indian folk rhythms with Caribbean soca energy — a genuinely Trinidadian genre with its own Chutney Soca Monarch competition.
  2. Powerful, syncopated drumming brought by Indian indentured labourers — heard at Hosay and festive Indo-Trinidadian occasions.
  3. Gentle Hindu devotional songs, sung at Divali gatherings and often blended with Caribbean rhythm as "Chutney-Bhajans."
  4. A softer steelpan or cuatro (small guitar) playlist for a mellow evening — a gentler side of Trinidadian music.
