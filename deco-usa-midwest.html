NORTH AMERICA / THE MIDWEST

Décor & Party Guide

USA: The Midwest

County fair lights and tailgate warmth, a Northwoods supper club by the lake, and Chicago's honest, no-fuss abundance.

01

The Visual Spirit

The Midwest's identity is honest, warm, and unpretentious — built by generations of German, Scandinavian, Polish, and Central European immigrants alongside the Great Migration that carried Southern soul food north. The result runs through three complementary registers. County fair and tailgate is string lights, folding tables, and communal abundance — the church potluck and the stadium parking lot, equally sacred. Supper club and lake cabin is Wisconsin's own genre entirely — dark wood, taxidermy, prime rib, and a brandy Old Fashioned, whether that's a Northwoods log-cabin lodge or a dimly-lit small-town diner-style club. Chicago and the big Midwestern city is deep dish, Italian beef, and fierce, funny local pride.

The Midwest's German-immigrant brewing and sausage heritage runs deep (see the dedicated Oktoberfest page for that full register) — here, the focus stays on what's distinctly, everyday Midwestern: county fairs, Friday fish fries, tailgates, and supper clubs. Keep the palette warm and harvest-toned, the materials plain wood and gingham, and the mood generous rather than flashy.

02

Setting the Scene

For a county fair/tailgate party, string warm lights, lay out gingham or plaid cloths, and set up a proper spread — corn dogs, pretzels, and cheese curds alongside a folding-table buffet. Team colours (especially green and gold for a Packers nod) and a cooler of beer complete the tailgate lot feel.

For a supper-club/lake-cabin evening, go dark wood, low warm lighting, and a bit of Northwoods lodge charm — a fireplace or its glow, simple mounted "trophy" prints rather than real taxidermy if that's not your thing, and a proper Brandy Old Fashioned bar. Friday-night fish fry (battered lake fish, coleslaw, tartar sauce) makes an authentic, easy centrepiece.

For a Chicago/city-food night, lean into deep-dish pizza and Italian beef as the meal's stars, with bold, simple graphic signage (diner-style) and a "no ketchup on the hot dog" wink for anyone who knows.

03

Dressing the Room

  • Fair/tailgate: string warm lights and lay out gingham or plaid cloths for that county-fair, folding-table warmth.
  • Fair/tailgate: bring in team colours — Packers green and gold, or your local favourite — for tailgate spirit.
  • Supper club: go dark wood and low warm lighting, with Northwoods-lodge touches — a fireplace glow, simple "trophy" prints.
  • Supper club: set up a proper Brandy Old Fashioned bar — Wisconsin's signature cocktail, made "the right way."
  • Chicago: feature deep-dish pizza and Italian beef as the meal's stars — proudly, fiercely local dishes.
  • Chicago: add bold diner-style signage — simple, graphic lettering with real local character.
  • Any register: bring in sweet corn, wheat sheaves, or harvest produce — a nod to the Midwest's agricultural heart.
  • Any register: keep the spread generous and unfussy — the Midwestern table is meant to fill people up.

Did You Know?

  • The Wisconsin supper club is its own genre entirely — Northwoods log-cabin lodges with taxidermy and prize fish, dimly-lit small-town diner-style rooms, and church-basement-chic mash-ups all count, and locals know exactly which category their favourite falls into.
  • The Friday night fish fry began as a Catholic tradition of abstaining from meat, kept alive through Prohibition when taverns sold fish plates to stay afloat — by the 1960s it had become embedded in Wisconsin life regardless of religious observance.
  • Green Bay Packers season tickets are passed down through families like heirlooms, and the team remains the only publicly owned franchise in major American sports — tailgating there is treated with near-religious devotion, even in sub-zero weather.
  • Chicago deep dish was invented at Pizzeria Uno in 1943, and locals actually eat thin crust more often day-to-day — deep dish is treated more like a special occasion than an everyday meal.
Décor & Ambiance Reference

Three registers, one heartland — county fair, supper club, and Chicago pride.

06

Room & Table Décor

Fair & Tailgate

  1. Warm string lights and red-and-white gingham cloths — the classic, cheerful look of a county fair or church picnic.
  2. Local team colours — Packers green and gold is the classic Midwest example — for genuine tailgate pride.
  3. Sweet corn, wheat sheaves, and pumpkins as centrepiece — a nod to the Midwest's enormous agricultural output.
  4. A generous, unfussy buffet spread — the church-potluck and county-fair ideal of feeding everyone well.

Supper Club & Chicago

  1. Dark wood panelling, low warm light, and simple lodge-style touches — the classic Wisconsin Northwoods supper club atmosphere.
  2. Brandy, bitters, and muddled fruit — Wisconsin's own take on the Old Fashioned, and a genuine centrepiece for any supper-club evening.
  3. Chicago's two most fiercely defended dishes, served proudly as the centrepiece of a city-food night.
  4. Simple, bold lettering in classic diner style — an easy way to add real Midwestern-city character.
07

What to Wear

Fair & Tailgate

  1. A local team jersey — Packers green and gold or your own favourite — the easiest, most genuine Midwestern party outfit.
  2. A simple flannel shirt — practical, comfortable, and right at home at a fair or a fish fry alike.
  3. A touch of blaze orange — a playful nod to hunting season, which is nearly a holiday across much of the Midwest.

Supper Club & City

  1. A step-up-from-casual outfit — supper clubs are a small occasion, dressier than a tailgate but still unpretentious.
  2. Chicago sports gear or simple city-pride clothing — straightforward and instantly recognisable.
08

Music & Sound

Heartland & Roots

  1. Straightforward, guitar-driven heartland rock — plainspoken and proud, much like the region itself.
  2. A cheerful legacy of the region's German, Polish, and Czech immigrant communities — still played at county fairs and weddings.
  3. Popular country radio — a near-universal soundtrack for a Midwestern tailgate or backyard gathering.

Chicago & Blues

  1. The electrified blues sound that made Chicago famous — soulful, gritty, and historically vital.
  2. Chicago is the birthplace of house music — an energetic, danceable option for a modern city-night playlist.
  3. Detroit's iconic soul sound — smooth, joyful, and one of America's greatest musical exports.
