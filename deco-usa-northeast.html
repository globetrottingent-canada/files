NORTH AMERICA / THE NORTHEAST

Décor & Party Guide

USA: Northeast

Nautical New England blue-and-white, the electric hum of a New York deli counter, and Philadelphia's fierce, proud corner-of-the-city character.

01

The Visual Spirit

The Northeast is the oldest European-settled corner of America and its greatest immigrant gateway, and its identity splits into three genuinely distinct moods. New England is nautical blue-and-white — shingled cottages, sailboats, lobster traps, and crisp coastal restraint, warming into wood-panelled cosiness and apple-cider colour once autumn arrives. New York City is the electric, accumulated energy of the immigrant deli and pizza-by-the-slice counter — block-letter neon, subway tile, and a hundred cultures layered into one skyline. Philadelphia and the Mid-Atlantic carry their own fierce, specific local pride — rowhouse brick, soft pretzels, and a cheesesteak argument that's lasted decades.

Whichever register you lean into, the materials stay honest — weathered wood, subway tile, exposed brick, and navy stripe — and the palette runs from nautical blue and white through deli-neon red and gold to Philly brick red and pretzel-salt brown.

02

Setting the Scene

For New England coastal style, go navy-and-white with nautical touches — rope, anchors, sea glass, and shiplap panelling. Set up a proper clam bake or lobster roll station, and if the season allows, lean into autumn: apple cider, maple, and fall foliage colour for a cosier version of the same coastal restraint.

For a New York deli/pizza night, lean into the "accumulated, not decorated" feel — subway tile, wood panelling, and a block-letter neon sign if you can manage one. Set up a proper deli spread (pastrami, bagels and lox, pickles) or a pizza-by-the-slice station, and don't over-style it; the real thing is unpretentious by design.

For Philadelphia/Mid-Atlantic pride, bring in brick-red tones, a soft pretzel display, and — the inevitable centrepiece — a proper cheesesteak bar, with the classic Whiz-vs-provolone debate as a fun conversation starter.

Did You Know?

  • The New England vs. Manhattan clam chowder rivalry reflects a genuine cultural divide so deep that a 1939 Maine state bill actually proposed making it illegal to add tomatoes to clam chowder — it never passed.
  • The New York deli has a defining look almost by accident — as one design writer put it, "a true New York deli isn't decorated, it's accumulated" — block-letter neon, subway tile, and wood panelling built up over generations rather than styled all at once.
  • New York-style pizza was created by Gennaro Lombardi in 1905 at America's first pizzeria; pizza makers still genuinely credit New York's soft, low-mineral water as part of what makes the dough special.
  • The Philly cheesesteak was invented by a hot dog vendor in 1930, and the rivalry between Pat's King of Steaks and Geno's Steaks — literally across the street from each other — has run for decades with no sign of resolving.
Décor & Ambiance Reference

Three registers, one region — coastal New England, New York energy, and Philly pride.

06

Room & Table Décor

New England

  1. Navy-and-white stripes, rope, anchors, and shiplap panelling — the crisp, timeless look of a New England coastal home.
  2. Fresh lobster, clams, and corn — the definitive New England summer spread, simply prepared and generously served.
  3. Collected sea glass and shells as small accents — a genuine, low-effort nod to the coastal New England tradition.
  4. Apple cider, maple, and fall-coloured leaves — a cosy, seasonal alternative to the crisp summer nautical look.

New York & Philadelphia

  1. A proper deli spread — pastrami on rye, bagels and lox, pickles — the heart of New York's Jewish deli heritage.
  2. White subway tile and a block-letter neon sign — the unpretentious, "accumulated not decorated" look of a classic NYC eatery.
  3. A proper cheesesteak setup — griddled beef, melted cheese, hoagie rolls — the fiercely defended centrepiece of Philadelphia food culture.
  4. Exposed brick tones and a warm soft-pretzel display — simple, comforting, and genuinely Philadelphian.
07

What to Wear

New England

  1. A navy-and-white striped top — the effortless, preppy-coastal uniform of a New England summer.
  2. Classic boat shoes and simple chinos — the relaxed, put-together look of the New England coast.
  3. Cosy flannel and wool for the fall version of New England style — warm, practical, and timeless.

New York & Philadelphia

  1. Simple, comfortable city clothing — New York style prizes practicality and confidence over costume.
  2. Philadelphia sports team gear — worn with the same fierce pride as the cheesesteak debate.
08

Music & Sound

New England

  1. Traditional sea shanties and New England folk music — a nod to the region's deep maritime history.
  2. Classic Boston-area rock bands — an easy, upbeat regional playlist.

New York & Philadelphia

  1. New York's storied jazz tradition — sophisticated, atmospheric, and endlessly city-appropriate.
  2. The lush, orchestrated soul sound that made Philadelphia a major music capital in the 1970s.
  3. New York City's foundational role in hip-hop — an energetic, essential addition to any city-themed playlist.
